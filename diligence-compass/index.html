      Method

      How the compass works

      Most deal models answer "what is the return?" The compass answers a different question: which unknown should the team spend its next week on?

      • Confidence becomes a range. Each assumption's tag sets its P10–P90 band. High, medium and low are ±2, ±5 and ±10 points on growth, and ±0.75x, ±1.5x and ±3x on the exit multiple. A typed range overrides the tag and can be lopsided.
      • The deal is simulated. Revenue, margin, cash conversion and debt paydown run year by year to an exit. Risks fire at their stated chance. Growth and the exit multiple can move together, because the same market drives both.
      • Every outcome is valued at your hurdle. Equity at exit, discounted at the hurdle rate, less the cheque. A positive average means the deal clears the hurdle at this price.
      • Value of information. For each unknown, the compass estimates how much the invest-or-pass call would improve, on average, if that unknown were known before signing. This is the expected value of partial perfect information, used to rank research in drug funding and oil exploration. It is estimated by regressing deal value on each unknown, following Strong, Oakley & Brennan (2014, Medical Decision Making).
      • A ceiling, not a forecast. Real diligence gives a partial answer, so treat each figure as the most a check could be worth, and compare it with the check's cost. Values inside simulation noise, or below 0.1% of the equity cheque, show as ≈ 0.

      What the model leaves out

      • No dividends, recaps or add-on acquisitions during the hold; one exit at the end.
      • Debt is sized in turns of headline EBITDA and repaid from free cash flow; the interest tax shield is ignored.
      • Risks hit at exit, with revenue effects phased in over the hold. One-off cash costs add to net debt at exit.
      • Equity can't go below zero, which is why the outcome range has a long right tail and a hard floor.
      Diligence Compass runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you type is sent anywhere. Model outputs depend on your own ranges; they are not forecasts or investment advice.
